\title{Cooperative and Non-Cooperative Coverage Costs for Urban BVLOS Surveillance}

\author{Pedro~Kukulka~de~Albuquerque and Andr\'{e}~R.~Kuroswiski

\markboth{IEEE Sensors Journal}{Albuquerque and Kuroswiski: Cooperative and Non-Cooperative Coverage Costs for Urban BVLOS}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Ground-based surveillance for urban Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) operations must cover two target classes: cooperative traffic, detectable by Remote~ID/RF receivers, and non-cooperative dark targets, detectable only by Radar, electro-optical (EO), or acoustic sensors. We measure how the two coverage layers respond to capital expenditure (CapEx) when both are evaluated under the same ray-traced line-of-sight model on OpenStreetMap (OSM) buildings. Threat-weighted cooperative and non-cooperative coverage are kept as separate objectives against CapEx and searched with NSGA-II. On a 1\,km$\times$1\,km synthetic city, cooperative coverage exceeds \(0.96\) in every run, whereas the best non-cooperative coverage is \(0.41\)--\(0.51\) across three seeds; the joint floor \(0.85/0.35\) is met in two of three seeds, at \$383k and \$600k. Uniform random search with the same 608 evaluations reaches the same hypervolume and the same asymmetry, so the fronts are reachable trade-offs, not optimal designs. On a 30\,m Avenida Paulista corridor with 6,389 OSM buildings, five RF receivers reach \(0.93\) cooperative coverage for \$150k, but the best non-cooperative coverage is \(0.22\). The same stored city plans cover \(39\)--\(52\%\) of the threat-weighted 20\,m airway cooperatively, against \(92\)--\(99\%\) at 45 and 65\,m. Matched-CapEx ablations under the same ray-tracer show that RF alone saturates cooperative coverage with zero non-cooperative coverage, and that mixed Radar/EO/Acoustic/RF sets give the best non-cooperative coverage only at \$320k (\(47.5\%\) with hardware-only budgets, \(39.0\%\) with \$15k per site). A single fused coverage score therefore overstates dark-target protection.
\end{abstract}
