\begin{abstract}
Urban Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) operations require Ground-Based Surveillance Systems (GBSS) that separately account for \emph{cooperative} traffic (e.g., Remote~ID / RF) and \emph{non-cooperative} dark targets (Radar, EO, Acoustic). Collapsing both into a single coverage score systematically understates dark-target risk. This paper revisits city-scale sensor placement with the SCOPAS dual-layer framework: multi-objective NSGA-II optimizing threat-weighted \(M_{wp,coop}\), \(M_{wp,noncoop}\), and CapEx under deterministic 3D LoS occlusion on OSM geometry, with Acoustic as a low-cost non-cooperative modality and explicit planning floors (e.g., \(0.90/0.35\)).

On a 1\,km$\times$1\,km synthetic city, a dual-layer front of 32 solutions spans cooperative coverage \(0.07\)--\(0.96\) and non-cooperative coverage \(0.00\)--\(0.56\). Cooperative \(\geq90\%\) is reachable near \$247k while non-cooperative coverage stays near \(0.13\); joint floors near \(0.85/0.35\) cost about \$320k. Airport split fronts and a Paulista lite case reinforce the same asymmetry. Two strengthening controls close prior claim gaps under the \emph{same} ray-tracer: (A)~on enumerable crops \(n\in\{8,10,12\}\), a fixed-$k$ GA matches oracle \(M_c\) within \(0.0\)--\(0.3\)\,pp; (B)~at matched CapEx, RF-only wins lean/mid \(M_c\) budgets (\$31k/\$76k) and heterogeneous mixes only edge RF at \$150k---so heterogeneity is \emph{not} universally superior for classic coverage, while dual-layer fronts remain necessary for dark-target floors. We do \emph{not} claim city-scale optimality gaps. Code: \url{https://github.com/andrekuros/SCOPAS_Sensor_Placement}.
\end{abstract}
