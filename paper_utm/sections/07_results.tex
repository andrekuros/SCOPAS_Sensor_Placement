\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

We report dual-layer NSGA-II fronts under SCOPAS: maximize threat-weighted cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,coop}\) and non-cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) while minimizing CapEx (hardware + site activation). Non-cooperative sensors are Radar, EO, and Acoustic; RF contributes only to the cooperative layer.

\subsection{Synthetic city (1\,km$\times$1\,km, five critical assets)}

Configuration: resolution 30\,m, population 32, 18 generations, 4 cores, sensor budget 3--15, Acoustic enabled. The run produced 32 non-dominated solutions. Cooperative coverage spans \(0.074\)--\(0.963\); non-cooperative coverage spans \(0.003\)--\(0.563\).

\textbf{Finding 1 (layer asymmetry).}
Cooperative coverage \(\geq90\%\) is reachable near \$247k, but that individual only achieves \(M_{wp,noncoop}=0.134\). Across the three seeds the cheapest such plan costs \$90k--\$306k with non-cooperative coverage \(0.00\)--\(0.20\). Dark-target coverage above \(0.50\) requires Radar-heavy mixes above \$500k and still does not jointly clear a strict \(0.90/0.50\) box in this budget.

\textbf{Finding 2 (practical joint floor).}
Under floors \(0.90/0.35\), the front has no feasible member (closest: \(0.895/0.434\) at \$503k). Softening to \(0.85/0.35\) yields five feasible solutions; cheapest \$320k (4 Radar + 2 RF). Across three seeded reruns this floor costs \$383k--\$600k or is not reached (Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds}).

\subsubsection{Run-to-run spread}
\label{sec:results_seeds}

Table~\ref{tab:city_seeds} reruns the same configuration with seeds 0, 1, and 2. Maximum cooperative coverage is stable (\(0.964\)--\(0.966\)). Everything that depends on the non-cooperative layer varies: maximum \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) spans \(0.408\)--\(0.511\) (median \(0.415\)), and hypervolume spans \(0.251\)--\(0.267\) (median \(0.254\)). The \(0.85/0.35\) floor is met in two of three seeds, at \$383k and \$600k; seed~2 has no feasible member. Seed~0 is the only run that also meets \(0.90/0.35\) (\$383k). The front in Fig.~\ref{fig:city_pareto} has higher hypervolume (\(0.305\)) than every seeded rerun, so its \$320k floor cost is a favorable draw, not a typical outcome. At this budget (\(32\times18\)) the optimizer has not converged on the non-cooperative axis; Findings~1--2 should be read as ``reachable'' rather than ``cheapest possible''.

\textbf{Random search at the same budget.}
The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:city_seeds} replaces NSGA-II with uniform random search: 608 layouts per seed, with sensor count, sites (221 candidates), and types drawn uniformly, scored by the same evaluator. Its hypervolume (\(0.251\)--\(0.254\)) sits inside the NSGA-II range (\(0.251\)--\(0.267\)). Random search meets the \(0.85/0.35\) floor in all three seeds (\$423k--\$488k) and the \(0.90/0.35\) floor in all three (\$500k--\$582k); NSGA-II meets them in two and one seeds. NSGA-II finds cheaper cooperative-only plans in one seed (\$90k) but not consistently. Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence} tracks hypervolume against evaluations; the GA points use the surviving population at each checkpoint, the random points use all samples so far, which favors random search early on. Between 576 and 608 evaluations the GA curves still rise (by \(0.007\)--\(0.014\)) while the random curves are nearly flat (\(\leq0.002\)), which suggests, but does not show, that a larger budget would separate them. At this budget we therefore cannot claim that NSGA-II beats random search on the city front. The layer asymmetry does not depend on the optimizer: no random layout exceeds \(M_{wp,noncoop}=0.51\), while cooperative coverage reaches \(0.96\)--\(0.97\) in every seed.

\textbf{Finding 3 (the asymmetry repeats).}
The same coop-high / noncoop-low pattern appears on the airport split fronts and on Paulista (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenes}). Airport cooperative search reaches \(100\%\) cooperative coverage; its non-cooperative search tops out near \(45\%\). Paulista reaches \(94.7\%\) cooperative coverage and \(21.9\%\) non-cooperative coverage.

\subsection{Requirement-seeking demos (prior short runs)}

Target-seeking configs aiming at \(0.90/0.35\) and stretch \(0.90/0.50\) confirm the same structure: cooperative floors are inexpensive with RF; joint dark-target floors near \(35\%\) are the practical planning corridor; \(50\%\) noncoop with \(\geq90\%\) coop remains a stretch (best observed joint near \(0.957/0.445\)).

\subsection{Airport SJC split fronts}

Split optimization on the São José dos Campos airport scene (resolution 40\,m, population 24, 12 generations) isolates each layer.

\textbf{Cooperative front.} Twenty-four solutions; \(M_{wp,coop}\) saturates to \(1.0\). The cheapest plan with \(M_{wp,coop}\geq0.90\) costs \$61k (1 RF + 2 Acoustic) but yields \(M_{wp,noncoop}\approx0.004\). High-coop RF-heavy plans at \$188k still leave non-cooperative coverage near zero.

\textbf{Non-cooperative front.} Twenty-four Radar/EO/Acoustic solutions span \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) from \(0.032\) to \(0.445\). Best observed dark-target coverage is \(0.445\) at \$405k (5 Radar + 2 EO); no plan reaches a 70\% noncoop threshold in this budget.

This split is the operational punchline: a cooperative optimum is \emph{not} a dark-target plan. BVLOS safety cases that quote a single coverage percentage from RF-inclusive optimization overstate non-cooperative protection.

\subsection{Avenida Paulista}

The real corridor (6,389 buildings, 93 rooftop sites, two critical assets) was run at 30\,m voxels with population 40 and 20 generations, producing 40 non-dominated solutions (Fig.~\ref{fig:paulista_pareto}).\footnote{An earlier reduced run (40\,m voxels, population 24, 10 generations) gave the same qualitative picture: best cooperative \(0.970\), best non-cooperative \(0.254\). Values at different voxel sizes are not directly comparable.} Five RF receivers clear \(M_{wp,coop}=0.933\) at \$150k with zero non-cooperative coverage. Best cooperative coverage is \(0.947\) (\$516k). Best non-cooperative coverage is \(0.219\) (\$571k, seven Radars), with companion cooperative coverage \(0.692\). The best joint member reaches \(0.920/0.201\) at \$545k (6 RF + 5 Radar + 1 EO). No member meets \(0.85/0.35\) or even \(0.85/0.25\).

Relative to the synthetic city, the dense corridor compresses dark-target coverage much more than cooperative coverage: the non-cooperative ceiling falls from \(0.41\)--\(0.56\) (city runs) to \(0.22\), while cooperative coverage above \(0.90\) remains cheap. Paulista-class canyons need denser non-cooperative deployments, relaxed floors, or altitude policy; adding RF nodes does not help the dark-target layer.

\subsection{Control A: small-$n$ oracle vs.\ GA}
\label{sec:results_oracle}

Table~\ref{tab:oracle_comparison} reports Control~A under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol_oracle}. On fully enumerable instances (\(n{=}8,k{=}3\) and \(n{=}10,k{=}4\)), NSGA-II* matches the oracle \(M_c\) exactly (\(\Delta{=}0.0\)\,pp). At \(n{=}12,k{=}5\) (type-sampled oracle), the gap is \(0.3\)\,pp. Greedy is competitive; Random trails on the largest crop. Wall-clock for the oracle stays under a few seconds at this resolution because \(P_D\) grids are precomputed.

\textbf{Interpretation.} Where exhaustive search is feasible, the GA recovers near-oracle classic \(M_c\) under the same ray-tracer. We treat this as method validation on enumerable instances---\emph{not} as a city-scale optimality certificate for dual-layer fronts with \(n\gtrsim25\).

\subsection{Control B: matched CapEx modality ablation}
\label{sec:results_ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:modality_ablation} reports best classic \(M_c\) at three CapEx budgets. RF-only wins at \$31k (\(88.3\%\) vs.\ heterogeneous \(82.3\%\)) and \$76k (\(95.3\%\) vs.\ \(93.8\%\)). Heterogeneous only edges RF at \$150k (\(98.3\%\) vs.\ \(98.0\%\)). Acoustic-only and EO-only remain near zero / single-digit \(M_c\) at these budgets (short effective range under the \(0.8\) threshold); Radar-only is infeasible at \$31k and weak thereafter relative to RF for classic coverage.

\textbf{Interpretation.} For CapEx-matched classic \(M_c\), heterogeneity is \emph{not} universally superior: lean and mid budgets favor RF-dominant layouts. Heterogeneity becomes competitive only at higher CapEx. This softens any ``always heterogeneous'' claim. Dual-layer airport splits still show why RF-only plans fail dark-target floors---the modality story depends on whether the planning metric is cooperative \(M_c\) or joint coop/noncoop.

\subsection{Control C: dual-layer CapEx ablation}
\label{sec:results_dual_ablation}

Tables~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_coop}--\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_noncoop} report Control~C with hardware-only budgets. On the cooperative axis, RF-only wins or ties every budget (\$31k--\$320k) with \(M_{wp,coop}\) from \(89.3\%\) to \(99.6\%\), but companion noncoop stays at \(0\%\). On the non-cooperative axis, EO-only leads at \$31k/\$76k (\(7.2\%\)/\(17.2\%\)) and Radar-only at \$150k (\(33.7\%\)); the heterogeneous gate only ties these, because its best pack is the single-modality one. Heterogeneous wins outright at \$320k with \(M_{wp,noncoop}=47.5\%\) and companion coop \(82.6\%\).

\textbf{With site activation.} Table~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_sites} repeats Control~C with \$15k per occupied site. Fewer sensors fit each budget, so all values drop. RF-only still wins or ties the cooperative layer (\(76.2\%\) at \$31k, \(98.4\%\) at \$320k) with zero non-cooperative coverage. \textbf{Interpretation.} Dual-layer CapEx matching overturns any RF-only reading of Control~B: RF saturates coop while contributing nothing to dark targets. Heterogeneity is \emph{necessary for joint floors} near the \$320k band, with or without site fees, but not for lean classic \(M_c\). Lean non-cooperative budgets favor a single modality: EO without site fees, Radar once each site costs \$15k.
