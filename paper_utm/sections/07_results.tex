\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

Each front trades threat-weighted cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,coop}\) and non-cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) against CapEx (hardware + site activation). Non-cooperative sensors are Radar, EO, and Acoustic; RF contributes only to the cooperative layer. Fronts are the non-dominated sets an NSGA-II run reached at a fixed budget; Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds} shows that uniform random search reaches the same hypervolume.

\subsection{Synthetic city (1\,km$\times$1\,km, five critical assets)}

Configuration: resolution 30\,m, population 32, 18 generations, 4 cores, sensor budget 3--15, Acoustic enabled. The run produced 32 non-dominated solutions. Cooperative coverage spans \(0.074\)--\(0.963\); non-cooperative coverage spans \(0.003\)--\(0.563\).

\textbf{Finding 1 (layer asymmetry).}
Cooperative coverage \(\geq90\%\) is reachable near \$247k, but that individual only achieves \(M_{wp,noncoop}=0.134\). Across the three seeds the cheapest such plan costs \$90k--\$306k with non-cooperative coverage \(0.00\)--\(0.20\). Dark-target coverage above \(0.50\) requires Radar-heavy mixes above \$500k and still does not jointly clear a strict \(0.90/0.50\) box in this budget.

\textbf{Finding 2 (practical joint floor).}
Under floors \(0.90/0.35\), the front has no feasible member (closest: \(0.895/0.434\) at \$503k). Softening to \(0.85/0.35\) yields five feasible solutions; cheapest \$320k (4 Radar + 2 RF). Across three seeded reruns this floor costs \$383k--\$600k or is not reached (Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds}).

\subsubsection{Run-to-run spread}
\label{sec:results_seeds}

Table~\ref{tab:city_seeds} reruns the same configuration with seeds 0, 1, and 2. Maximum cooperative coverage is stable (\(0.964\)--\(0.966\)). Everything that depends on the non-cooperative layer varies: maximum \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) spans \(0.408\)--\(0.511\) (median \(0.415\)), and hypervolume spans \(0.251\)--\(0.267\) (median \(0.254\)). The \(0.85/0.35\) floor is met in two of three seeds, at \$383k and \$600k; seed~2 has no feasible member. Seed~0 is the only run that also meets \(0.90/0.35\) (\$383k). The front in Fig.~\ref{fig:city_pareto} has higher hypervolume (\(0.305\)) than every seeded rerun, so its \$320k floor cost is a favorable draw, not a typical outcome. At 608 evaluations, NSGA-II and uniform random search reach the same hypervolume (below); Findings~1--2 should be read as ``reachable'' rather than ``cheapest possible''.

\textbf{Random search at the same budget.}
The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:city_seeds} replaces NSGA-II with uniform random search: 608 layouts per seed, with sensor count, sites (221 candidates), and types drawn uniformly, scored by the same evaluator. Its hypervolume (\(0.251\)--\(0.254\)) sits inside the NSGA-II range (\(0.251\)--\(0.267\)). Random search meets the \(0.85/0.35\) floor in all three seeds (\$423k--\$488k) and the \(0.90/0.35\) floor in all three (\$500k--\$582k); NSGA-II meets them in two and one seeds. NSGA-II finds cheaper cooperative-only plans in one seed (\$90k) but not consistently. Tracking hypervolume against evaluations, between 576 and 608 evaluations the NSGA-II values still rise (by \(0.007\)--\(0.014\)) while the random-search values are nearly flat (\(\leq0.002\)), which suggests, but does not show, that a larger budget would separate them. At this budget we therefore cannot claim that NSGA-II beats random search on the city front. The layer asymmetry does not depend on the optimizer: no random layout exceeds \(M_{wp,noncoop}=0.51\), while cooperative coverage reaches \(0.96\)--\(0.97\) in every seed.

\textbf{Finding 3 (the asymmetry repeats).}
The same pattern appears on the airport split fronts and on Paulista (Fig.~\ref{fig:scenes}; Sections~\ref{sec:results_airport}--\ref{sec:results_paulista}).

\subsection{Airport SJC split fronts}
\label{sec:results_airport}

Split optimization on the São José dos Campos airport scene (resolution 40\,m, population 24, 12 generations) isolates each layer.

\textbf{Cooperative front.} Twenty-four solutions; \(M_{wp,coop}\) saturates to \(1.0\). The cheapest plan with \(M_{wp,coop}\geq0.90\) costs \$61k (1 RF + 2 Acoustic) but yields \(M_{wp,noncoop}\approx0.004\). High-coop RF-heavy plans at \$188k still leave non-cooperative coverage near zero.

\textbf{Non-cooperative front.} Twenty-four Radar/EO/Acoustic solutions span \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) from \(0.032\) to \(0.445\). Best observed dark-target coverage is \(0.445\) at \$405k (5 Radar + 2 EO); no plan reaches a 70\% noncoop threshold in this budget.

The split shows that the cheapest cooperative plan is not a dark-target plan. A BVLOS safety case that quotes one coverage percentage from an RF-inclusive layout overstates non-cooperative protection.

\subsection{Avenida Paulista}
\label{sec:results_paulista}

The real corridor (6,389 buildings, 93 rooftop sites, two critical assets) was run at 30\,m voxels with population 40 and 20 generations, producing 40 non-dominated solutions.\footnote{An earlier reduced run (40\,m voxels, population 24, 10 generations) gave the same qualitative picture: best cooperative \(0.970\), best non-cooperative \(0.254\). Values at different voxel sizes are not directly comparable.} Five RF receivers clear \(M_{wp,coop}=0.933\) at \$150k with zero non-cooperative coverage. Best cooperative coverage is \(0.947\) (\$516k). Best non-cooperative coverage is \(0.219\) (\$571k, seven Radars), with companion cooperative coverage \(0.692\). The best joint member reaches \(0.920/0.201\) at \$545k (6 RF + 5 Radar + 1 EO). No member meets \(0.85/0.35\) or even \(0.85/0.25\).

Relative to the synthetic city, the dense corridor lowers the non-cooperative ceiling from \(0.41\)--\(0.56\) to \(0.22\), while cooperative coverage above \(0.90\) remains cheap; adding RF nodes does not help the dark-target layer.

\subsection{Control A: small-$n$ oracle vs.\ GA}
\label{sec:results_oracle}

Table~\ref{tab:oracle_comparison} reports Control~A under the protocol of Section~\ref{sec:protocol_oracle}. On fully enumerable instances (\(n{=}8,k{=}3\) and \(n{=}10,k{=}4\)), NSGA-II* matches the oracle \(M_c\) exactly (\(\Delta{=}0.0\)\,pp). At \(n{=}12,k{=}5\) (type-sampled oracle), the gap is \(0.3\)\,pp. Greedy is competitive; Random trails on the largest crop. Oracle wall-clock stays under a few seconds because \(P_D\) grids are precomputed. This validates the search on enumerable instances; it is not a city-scale optimality certificate for dual-layer fronts with \(n\gtrsim25\).

\subsection{Control B: matched CapEx modality ablation}
\label{sec:results_ablation}

Fig.~\ref{fig:controls}(a) reports best classic \(M_c\) at three CapEx budgets. RF-only wins at \$31k (\(88.3\%\) vs.\ heterogeneous \(82.3\%\)) and \$76k (\(95.3\%\) vs.\ \(93.8\%\)). Heterogeneous only edges RF at \$150k (\(98.3\%\) vs.\ \(98.0\%\)). Acoustic-only and EO-only remain near zero / single-digit \(M_c\) at these budgets (short effective range under the \(0.8\) threshold); Radar-only is infeasible at \$31k and weak thereafter relative to RF for classic coverage.

For CapEx-matched classic \(M_c\), heterogeneity is therefore not universally superior; whether it helps depends on whether the planning metric is cooperative \(M_c\) or joint coop/noncoop coverage (Control~C).

\subsection{Control C: dual-layer CapEx ablation}
\label{sec:results_dual_ablation}

Tables~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_coop}--\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_noncoop} report Control~C with hardware-only budgets. On the cooperative axis, RF-only wins or ties every budget (\$31k--\$320k) with \(M_{wp,coop}\) from \(89.3\%\) to \(99.6\%\), but companion noncoop stays at \(0\%\). On the non-cooperative axis, EO-only leads at \$31k/\$76k (\(7.2\%\)/\(17.2\%\)) and Radar-only at \$150k (\(33.7\%\)); the heterogeneous gate only ties these, because its best pack is the single-modality one. Heterogeneous wins outright at \$320k with \(M_{wp,noncoop}=47.5\%\) and companion coop \(82.6\%\).

\textbf{With site activation.} Table~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_sites} repeats Control~C with \$15k per occupied site. Fewer sensors fit each budget, so all values drop. RF-only still wins or ties the cooperative layer (\(76.2\%\) at \$31k, \(98.4\%\) at \$320k) with zero non-cooperative coverage. On the non-cooperative layer, Acoustic leads at \$31k (\(1.6\%\)), Radar-only at \$76k and \$150k (\(14.0\%\), \(24.9\%\)), and Heterogeneous wins only at \$320k (\(39.0\%\), companion coop \(70\%\)).

Site fees penalize many cheap sensors relative to a few long-range ones, so the lean non-cooperative leader moves from EO to Radar. RF saturates the cooperative layer while contributing nothing to dark targets, and mixed sets are needed only for joint floors near the \$320k band, with or without site fees.
