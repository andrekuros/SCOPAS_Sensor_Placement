\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the dual-layer fronts change}

A single fused coverage percentage hides the layer that is hard to buy. On every scene we report, cooperative \(M_{wp,coop}\) saturates with RF-inclusive mixes at modest CapEx, while non-cooperative \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) stays incomplete at several hundred thousand dollars. The airport split makes the failure explicit: the cheapest plan that clears \(0.90\) cooperative coverage (\$61k) leaves dark-target coverage near zero. A BVLOS safety case should therefore report both layers, or a dual-layer front with stated floors, rather than one number.

\subsection{Altitude is a first-order lever}

Fig.~\ref{fig:airways} evaluates three stored city solutions per airway without re-optimizing. For the same hardware, cooperative \(M_{wp}\) on the lowest airway is \(39\)--\(52\%\), against \(92\)--\(99\%\) at 45 and 65\,m. The non-cooperative layer drops more: the best dark-target plan (\$565k) covers \(35\%\) of the threat-weighted 20\,m layer and \(72\%\) at 45\,m; the \$320k joint plan falls from \(47\%\) to \(15\%\). Placing BVLOS traffic above the low-rise skyline buys more coverage than any hardware increment on the front. Corridors that must stay near 20\,m need denser or lower-mounted non-cooperative sensors, which this study does not optimize for.

\subsection{Site activation changes the modality ranking}

Control~C was first run with hardware-only budgets (Tables~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_coop}--\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_noncoop}). Charging \$15k per occupied site (Table~\ref{tab:modality_ablation_dual_sites}) penalizes many cheap sensors relative to a few long-range ones. RF-only cooperative coverage at \$31k falls from \(89.3\%\) to \(76.2\%\). On the non-cooperative layer, EO no longer leads at lean budgets; Radar-only leads at \$76k and \$150k (\(14.0\%\), \(24.9\%\)). Heterogeneous mixes still win only at \$320k, now at \(39.0\%\) with companion cooperative coverage \(70\%\), against \(47.5\%\) and \(83\%\) without site fees. The heterogeneity claim therefore survives site costs, but only in the joint-floor budget band.

\subsection{What the evidence does not show}

We do not claim optimality on the city-scale fronts. Control~A bounds GA quality on enumerable crops (\(n\leq12\)) and for classic \(M_c\) only. Controls~B--C search cropped site pools (36 and 28 sites) with sampled site subsets; differences of \(0.2\)\,pp or less between a single-modality gate and the heterogeneous gate in those tables are sampling noise, not evidence for either. Three seeded reruns of the city front (Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds}) all have lower hypervolume than the front in Fig.~\ref{fig:city_pareto}, and reach the \(0.85/0.35\) floor in only two of three runs. At the budgets used here, single-run floor costs are reachable costs, not cheapest-possible costs; the qualitative asymmetry, by contrast, holds in every run.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Binary line of sight.} \(P_{LoS}\in\{0,1\}\) ignores diffraction, reflection, and penetration. Coverage is a worst-case geometric estimate and may understate real detection in street canyons.
\item \textbf{One real corridor.} Paulista is a single corridor evaluated at 30\,m voxels with one optimizer run. Transfer to other urban morphologies is not tested.
\item \textbf{Cropped control pools.} Controls~B--C use cropped site pools and sampled site subsets. They support matched-budget modality comparisons, not statements about the full NSGA-II fronts.
\item \textbf{No field calibration.} Sensor \(P_D\) models and CapEx figures are nominal. No instrumented corridor anchors the absolute coverage values; relative comparisons under the same models are the supported use.
\item \textbf{Coarse vertical resolution.} At 30\,m voxels, the 20\,m airway is evaluated on the 0--30\,m layer, so the low-airway values in Fig.~\ref{fig:airways} average over that band.
\end{enumerate}
