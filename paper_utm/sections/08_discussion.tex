\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the dual-layer fronts change}

A single fused coverage percentage hides the layer that is hard to buy: on every scene, cooperative coverage saturates at modest CapEx while non-cooperative coverage stays incomplete at several hundred thousand dollars. A BVLOS safety case should therefore report both layers, or a dual-layer front with stated floors, rather than one number.

\subsection{Altitude is a first-order lever}

For the same stored hardware (Fig.~\ref{fig:airways}), moving from the 20\,m airway to 45\,m roughly doubles cooperative coverage and more than doubles non-cooperative coverage. Placing BVLOS traffic above the low-rise skyline buys more coverage than any hardware increment on the front. Corridors that must stay near 20\,m need denser or lower-mounted non-cooperative sensors, which this study does not optimize for.

\subsection{What the evidence does not show}

We do not claim optimality on the city-scale fronts. Control~A bounds GA quality on enumerable crops (\(n\leq12\)) and for classic \(M_c\) only. Controls~B--C search cropped site pools (36 and 28 sites) with sampled site subsets; differences of \(0.2\)\,pp or less between a single-modality gate and the heterogeneous gate in those tables are sampling noise, not evidence for either. The three seeded city reruns (Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds}) have lower hypervolume than the front in Fig.~\ref{fig:city_pareto}, and uniform random search with the same 608 evaluations matches their hypervolume, so at this budget NSGA-II shows no advantage over random sampling. Single-run floor costs are reachable costs, not cheapest-possible costs; the qualitative asymmetry, by contrast, holds in every run and under both search procedures.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Binary line of sight.} \(P_{LoS}\in\{0,1\}\) ignores diffraction, reflection, and penetration. Coverage is a worst-case geometric estimate and may understate real detection in street canyons.
\item \textbf{One real corridor.} Paulista is a single corridor evaluated at 30\,m voxels with one optimizer run. Transfer to other urban morphologies is not tested.
\item \textbf{Cropped control pools.} Controls~B--C use cropped site pools and sampled site subsets. They support matched-budget modality comparisons, not statements about the full NSGA-II fronts.
\item \textbf{No field calibration.} Sensor \(P_D\) models and CapEx figures are nominal. No instrumented corridor anchors the absolute coverage values; relative comparisons under the same models are the supported use.
\item \textbf{Coarse vertical resolution.} At 30\,m voxels, the 20\,m airway is evaluated on the 0--30\,m layer, so the low-airway values in Fig.~\ref{fig:airways} average over that band.
\end{enumerate}
