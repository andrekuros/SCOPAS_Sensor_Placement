\section{Introduction}

Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) drone operations and Urban Air Mobility (UAM) need continuous surveillance to support Detect-and-Avoid for manned and unmanned aircraft \cite{faa2023uam,mueller2017enabling}. Unlike manned traffic with transponders, BVLOS airspace includes uncooperative users---malfunctioning drones, hobbyist UAVs, unauthorized vehicles---that must still be detected \cite{faa2020utm}. Surveillance reliability limits capacity: regulators may impose separation minima of about 500\,m when detection is uncertain, whereas high-confidence monitoring can reduce separations by an order of magnitude \cite{nasa2021utm}.

Ground-Based Surveillance Systems (GBSS) provide this monitoring layer for UTM \cite{kopardekar2016unmanned}. Radar works in all weather and lighting but struggles with small radar cross-section UAVs at range \cite{guvenc2018detection,semkin2020analyzing}. RF/Remote~ID receivers are cheap but detect only cooperative transmitters \cite{guvenc2018detection,besada2022cuas}. EO/IR sensors give high-resolution coverage but degrade in fog, rain, or darkness \cite{guvenc2018detection,yan2023passive}. Acoustic sensors are low-cost non-cooperative detectors of propeller noise but are highly susceptible to urban background noise \cite{guvenc2018detection,yan2023passive}; sound can partly propagate around obstacles, but this study applies the same line-of-sight gate as for Radar, RF, and EO (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}). Heterogeneous networks combine these modalities \cite{muscat2023,besada2022cuas,blasch2021information}, and their placement must balance coverage, budget, rooftop access, and line-of-sight obstruction \cite{aievola2022ground,dulia2024sand}.

Prior GBSS placement work (Section~\ref{sec:related}, Table~\ref{tab:related_comparison}) leaves three gaps. \emph{Scalability}: exhaustive and many MIP formulations become impractical for tens of heterogeneous sensors over square kilometers with occlusion physics \cite{muscat2023,katoh1998resource}. \emph{Urban fidelity}: statistical LoS models omit the building-resolved shadowing that dominates low airways \cite{3gpp38901,itur_p1411}, and OSM-based deterministic LoS remains rare in C-UAS CapEx planning \cite{osm_applications}. \emph{Metric split}: cooperative and non-cooperative coverage are often collapsed into one score, understating dark-target risk for BVLOS safety cases \cite{besada2022cuas,blasch2021information}.

The question we answer is how far cooperative and non-cooperative coverage diverge under matched CapEx and the same occlusion model, and what that implies for BVLOS planning floors. The contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Separate coverage layers under CapEx}: threat-weighted cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,coop}\) and non-cooperative coverage \(M_{wp,noncoop}\) are kept as separate objectives against CapEx (hardware plus site activation), under deterministic ray-traced line of sight on OSM buildings, instead of one fused coverage score.
    \item \textbf{The asymmetry across scenes and altitudes}: cooperative coverage saturates with RF-inclusive mixes at modest CapEx while non-cooperative coverage stays incomplete on a synthetic city (three seeds), an airport split, and the Avenida Paulista corridor; per-airway re-evaluation shows the same plans cover far less at 20\,m than at 45--65\,m.
    \item \textbf{Controls that bound the modality claim}: under the same ray-tracer, (A)~a small-$n$ oracle comparison, (B)~a matched-CapEx classic \(M_c\) ablation, and (C)~a dual-layer CapEx ablation with and without site cost show when a mixed Radar/EO/Acoustic set is needed (joint dark-target floors) and when RF alone suffices (lean cooperative coverage).
\end{enumerate}

NSGA-II is the search procedure, not a contribution. We do not claim that the city-scale fronts are optimal: at 608 evaluations, NSGA-II and uniform random search reach the same hypervolume (Section~\ref{sec:results_seeds}).
